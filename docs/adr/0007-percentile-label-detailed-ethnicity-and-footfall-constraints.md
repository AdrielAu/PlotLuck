# Percentile label, detailed ethnicity and rising footfall

On 2026-10-18 the user reviewed the live NN1 and found three problems. Scores bunched between about 0.62 and 0.76 for every profile. A higher residential population lowered every score, so sparsely populated districts ranked best. A larger Asian share slightly lowered the score for an Indian restaurant. Bundle version 5 makes four changes.

**Label.** The ADR 0002 composite (0.50·R_s + 0.30·D_s + 0.20·V) is unchanged, but the training target is now the composite's percentile among the training rows. The policy keeps 1,001 quantiles of the training composites, and labels are interpolated between them. Ties share their mean position, and values outside the training range clip to 0 or 1. Labels therefore spread evenly over 0–1, and a score reads as "this kind of restaurant here is expected to out-review X% of restaurants". Each CV fold refits the quantiles on its own training rows. Held-out MAE and RMSE are on the new scale, so they cannot be compared with runs before version 5; compare each run against its own baseline.

**Detailed ethnicity.** NN1 reads TS021's 19 detailed ethnic groups (for example Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Chinese, African, Caribbean, Arab, Irish, Other White) instead of its five broad totals. The broad totals are sums of the detailed groups, so they are no longer read. `most_common_ethnicity` now names a detailed group. The allocation from 2021 MSOAs is unchanged (ADR 0005).

**Matched community.** The new district input `matched_community_share` is the share of residents in the ethnic groups matched to the profile's fine cuisine. The mapping is in `src/plotluck/cuisine_communities.csv` and is exported in `vocabulary.json`. Examples: Indian → Indian + Pakistani + Bangladeshi, Caribbean → Caribbean + White and Black Caribbean, Polish → Other White. An unmatched cuisine (Seafood, American) gets 0. The mapping is a product judgement: Census ethnic groups do not identify national cuisines one to one.

**Rising inputs.** Training projects the weights so that the score never falls as `population`, `total_restaurants`, `attractions_count`, `hotels_count` or `matched_community_share` rises. Those columns' first-layer weights and every later-layer weight stay non-negative. This is a guarantee for any input, not a soft penalty, and the browser forward pass is unchanged. Other inputs (deprivation, rent, age, sex, same-cuisine supply, each ethnic share) keep a learned direction of either sign.

The constraint means a factor can raise a score or have no effect, but never lower it. It cannot make a factor matter more than the reviews support. Where the data say a factor lowers reviews, the trained effect is pushed to zero, and correlated unconstrained inputs (age, White British share, deprivation) may absorb the same rural signal. Check the full-data run with `python -m plotluck.ranking_audit` and the representative urban, rural and low-population districts in [RANKING.md](../competition/RANKING.md) before publishing.

Bundle version 5 must be trained on LinuxONE from the private rows (ADR 0003). The committed sample uses synthetic context and establishes only that the path runs. The site reads versions 2–5, so the published version 4 weights keep working until a version 5 export passes its gates.
