# Add district demographics and tourism context to NN1

On 2026-10-17 the team added average age, ethnicity shares, Census sex shares and separate attraction and hotel counts to NN1. Population shares preserve mixed districts better than a most-common category alone. `most_common_ethnicity` and `most_common_sex` remain descriptive summaries, with `tie` and `unknown` values; only the numeric shares enter NN1. Census sex (female/male) is distinct from gender identity. These are aggregate resident characteristics, not individual customer preferences.

The ten added numeric inputs are `average_age`, `ethnicity_asian_share`, `ethnicity_black_share`, `ethnicity_mixed_share`, `ethnicity_white_share`, `ethnicity_other_share`, `sex_female_share`, `sex_male_share`, `attractions_count` and `hotels_count`. Shares use the five broad, non-overlapping Census ethnic groups and the two Census sex categories. Age and shares are standardised without a logarithm; tourism counts use log1p then standardisation. Missing demographics retain empty cells until training-fold median imputation. No new restaurant picker is needed. Architecture and the review-based target are unchanged.

Sources are Census 2021 [TS007](https://www.nomisweb.co.uk/datasets/c2021ts007), [TS021](https://www.nomisweb.co.uk/datasets/c2021ts021), [TS008](https://www.nomisweb.co.uk/datasets/c2021ts008), and OpenStreetMap `tourism=attraction` / `tourism=hotel`. TS007's bulk single-year ages stop at MSOA geography, so all three tables use 2021 MSOAs. ONSPD August 2026 links active English postcodes to 2021 MSOAs (`msoa21cd`); each MSOA's counts are allocated to districts in proportion to its active postcode counts. Counts are added before calculating the mean age or shares. This is an approximation that assumes residents are distributed equally across an MSOA's active postcodes; commercial postcodes and changes since 2021 can bias it. The 100+ age category is represented by 100. Cross-border context uses English postcodes only, whereas the existing P004 population may include Welsh residents. Replace this allocation with a resident-weighted crosswalk when available.

Tourism points are assigned to the nearest live English postcode, using a way/relation's centre. Each OSM type/ID counts once; a physical attraction mapped as distinct elements can still be counted more than once. These are mapped-object counts, not visitor counts, hotel capacity or a boundary-exact district inventory. A missing tourism extract is an error, while an extracted district with no mapped objects gets zero. Missing demographic extracts also fail rather than silently training without the new inputs.

The district CSV gains the ten numeric columns and two summary columns; bundle version becomes 2 so older consumers fail visibly. All fitted transforms remain inside CV training folds. The committed context fixtures and sample-trained weights use made-up, seeded context, explicitly marked in provenance; they establish functionality, not predictive improvement. Pull the public sources and retrain on LinuxONE, then compare district-grouped CV against the baseline and check x86 parity before shipping a full-data model.
